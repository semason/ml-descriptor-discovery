# ML-Assisted Descriptor Discovery for Materials Chemistry

This repository contains an interactive Google Colab notebook developed as a companion to the **[MoleCVUE – ACID Series](https://sites.google.com/view/molecvue/acidseries)** seminar:

**Thinking Like a Computational Materials Scientist: From Chemical Intuition to ML-Assisted Descriptor Discovery**

The notebook introduces a descriptor-based approach to understanding and predicting materials properties, using metal-release energetics in complex oxides as a model problem.

## About the notebook

A central challenge in computational materials science is determining which properties of a material contain the most useful information for predicting its behavior.

In this notebook, participants explore that question by testing different representations of the local chemical environment and asking how well they predict a target energetic property. The exercise progresses from chemically motivated descriptors toward machine-learning-assisted descriptor discovery.

Rather than treating machine learning simply as a prediction tool, the notebook emphasizes its use as a way to test physical hypotheses and identify relationships between material descriptors and calculated properties.

## What you will explore

The notebook examines several descriptor sets, including:

* structural and bond-valence descriptors;
* metal identity, oxidation state, and spin information; and
* electronic-structure descriptors associated with metal–oxygen hybridization.

The goal is not only to determine which descriptors improve prediction, but also to consider **why** they contain predictive information and what their successes and limitations reveal about the underlying materials chemistry.

## Educational context

This notebook was developed for an interactive seminar in the MoleCVUE ACID Series. It is intended as a teaching demonstration of the scientific reasoning involved in descriptor selection, model construction, and interpretation.

The dataset and workflow are adapted from published research, but the notebook uses a simplified analysis designed for interactive exploration. Results generated by the notebook should therefore not be interpreted as reproducing the quantitative results reported in the associated publication.
